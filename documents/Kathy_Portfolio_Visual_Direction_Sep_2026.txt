KATHY PORTFOLIO VISUAL DIRECTION
Operational design brief for a mid-level UX / Product Design portfolio
Updated: September 15, 2026


============================================================
01. PURPOSE
============================================================

This document is the source of truth for how Kathy's portfolio should look,
move, connect, and stage information.

It supersedes the earlier direction centered on a mostly calm editorial shell.
The new system keeps the rigor, readability, and project-specific worlds, but
raises the level of art direction.

The portfolio should not look like a sequence of tasteful content sections or
a stack of isolated campaign viewports. It should read like an authored online
magazine: one continuous argument with deliberately concentrated moments of
visual disruption.


============================================================
02. NORTH STAR
============================================================

PRIMARY DIRECTION

    STRUCTURED EDITORIAL DESIGN
    WITH EXPRESSIVE DISRUPTION

OPERATING PRINCIPLE

    CONTINUOUS EDITORIAL NARRATIVE
    WITH CONCENTRATED VISUAL DISRUPTION

    MAGAZINE FIRST.
    CAMPAIGN MOMENT SECOND.

SUPPORTING FORMULA

    EDITORIAL STORYTELLING
    + GRAPHIC-SYSTEM THINKING
    + PRODUCT RIGOR

POSITIONING INTENT

    Product-design rigor presented with creative-agency art direction.

The differentiating tension is:

    expressive visual craft x analytical product thinking

The desired impression is not merely:

    "A Product Designer with good visual taste."

It is:

    "A visual storyteller who thinks in product systems, behavior,
    evidence, and consequences."

Or, more bluntly:

    A graphic-designer brain operating inside product-design logic.


============================================================
03. THE PORTFOLIO'S JOB
============================================================

Within approximately five seconds, a recruiter should understand:

- Kathy is a Product Designer.
- She has shipped real work.
- She handles complex product systems and ambiguous problems.
- She has unusually strong visual craft and storytelling ability.
- CoachCare is the flagship professional proof.
- Summer Quest demonstrates ownership, behavioral thinking, and live learning.
- PerformaMatch demonstrates research synthesis and strategic framing.

After deeper reading, a hiring manager should understand:

- which decisions Kathy owned
- what evidence changed her thinking
- what tradeoffs she made
- what changed because of the work
- how she operates with constraints and collaborators
- what working with her might feel like

The visual system must accelerate that understanding. It must never make the
reader solve a design escape room to discover Kathy's job title.


============================================================
04. GOVERNANCE: WHAT CONTROLS WHAT
============================================================

THE PORTFOLIO SYSTEM CONTROLS

- navigation
- master grid
- typography roles
- spacing logic
- metadata and annotation behavior
- interaction principles
- accessibility
- content functions
- presentation scales
- continuous editorial spine
- recurring composition families

THE PROJECT SYSTEM CONTROLS

- project palette and atmosphere
- photography and image treatment
- project-specific graphic metaphor
- illustration language
- data-art direction
- intensity of expression

The shell creates recognition. The project creates character.

The structure must work in black and white. Project color adds atmosphere and
meaning; it does not carry hierarchy by itself.


============================================================
05. CORE VISUAL BEHAVIORS
============================================================

1. STRONG GRID, BROKEN DELIBERATELY

The grid should be rigorous enough that misalignment, overlap, cropping, and
edge-breaking feel intentional. Random placement is not expressive design.
It is merely evidence that CSS has won.


2. TYPOGRAPHY IS SPATIAL

Words may be oversized, cropped, stacked, repeated, faded, interrupted by
imagery, or placed across fields. Important line breaks should be composed,
not surrendered to accidental browser wrapping.

Typography can function as:

- narrative
- shape
- texture
- hierarchy
- transition
- navigation
- negative space


3. ARTIFACTS ARE STAGED

Screenshots, photographs, research fragments, diagrams, quotes, and metrics
should be treated like exhibits. They should be given scale, distance, and
context instead of being poured into identical cards.


4. EXTREME HIERARCHY

Use dramatic contrast:

- monumental claim vs. micro metadata
- full-field image vs. tiny caption
- dense evidence vs. deliberate emptiness
- hero metric vs. supporting interpretation

Avoid a page where every element lives at the same polite medium scale.


5. ONE DOMINANT IDEA PER EDITORIAL UNIT

Every meaningful unit needs a clear protagonist: one claim, artifact, metric,
image, or decision. The unit may be shorter or longer than a viewport and may
remain visibly connected to the units around it. Supporting material may be
complex, but the entry point must be obvious.

Do not confuse hierarchy with pagination. A dominant idea does not require a
full-screen enclosure.


6. DENSITY FLUCTUATES

The portfolio should move between sparse and dense states:

    statement -> evidence -> artifact -> clarity -> statement

Do not distribute visual intensity evenly. If every section performs a heroic
entrance, none of them matters.


7. FUNCTIONAL UI BEHAVES SIMPLY

Navigation, accordions, buttons, galleries, tabs, and forms should be direct,
predictable, and accessible. The surrounding composition carries expression.
Not every component needs a theatrical backstory.


8. IMAGERY MAY DOMINATE

When a photograph or artifact already proves the point, let it carry the beat.
Do not narrate over evidence that can speak for itself.


9. INTERRUPTION MUST HAVE A JOB

A line, crop, handwritten mark, offset label, floating image, or motion event
must clarify emphasis, sequence, relationship, or character. Decorative noise
is not a system.


10. EVERY PROJECT NEEDS A GRAPHIC METAPHOR

The strongest project world is built from one concept that can extend across:

- typography
- diagrams
- data
- imagery
- transitions
- motion
- interaction

Take the logic from references, not their costume.


============================================================
06. EXPRESSION AND PACING
============================================================

PORTFOLIO-WIDE CHARACTER

    approximately 75% rigorous / 25% unruly

Do not spread the 25% evenly. Concentration creates contrast.

CASE-STUDY PRESENTATION SCALE

    65–75%  RUNNING EDITORIAL FLOW
    20–30%  FEATURE COMPOSITIONS
     5–10%  IMMERSIVE MOMENTS

These percentages are directional, not quotas. Immersive moments are a maximum,
not an obligation. A chapter can succeed without one.

Summer Quest may use the highest case-study intensity. Even there, reserve
immersive treatment for the hero, a true reframe, and/or a major outcome.
CoachCare should use the same system with substantially more restraint.


============================================================
07. TWO AXES: CONTENT FUNCTION x PRESENTATION SCALE
============================================================

Do not treat content function and visual scale as the same decision. A statement
can be an inline feature. Product evidence can temporarily become immersive.
Choose each axis independently.


AXIS A — CONTENT FUNCTION

01. STATEMENT

Purpose:
- chapter thesis
- major reframe
- product principle
- hero outcome
- closing argument

Behavior:
- conclusion-led language
- one clear claim or number
- may use extreme scale contrast
- may remain embedded inside the article flow
- does not automatically require a full viewport


02. EDITORIAL EVIDENCE

Purpose:
- research insight
- behavior pattern
- decision rationale
- quote + context
- metric + interpretation
- comparison or turning point

Behavior:
- evidence and explanation share one unit
- captions and annotations remain attached to their source
- visual hierarchy reflects claim -> evidence -> meaning
- may use a project-specific graphic device


03. PRODUCT CLARITY

Purpose:
- explain what was designed
- compare before / after
- show an interaction or state change
- document product architecture
- make a complex workflow understandable

Behavior:
- controlled reading order
- readable body copy
- legible screens
- explicit annotation
- restrained motion
- minimal overlap


AXIS B — PRESENTATION SCALE

01. RUNNING

The default online-magazine flow. Text, evidence, images, quotes, and captions
share one continuous article spine. Units size themselves to their content,
not to the viewport.

02. FEATURE

A stronger composition within the continuous article. It may expand across the
grid, change background, enlarge typography, or stage an artifact, but it keeps
visual continuity with what comes before and after.

03. IMMERSIVE

A rare hero-scale event used for a project opening, decisive reframe, major
outcome, or closing argument. It may approach or occupy a viewport when the idea
has earned that scale.

The case study should usually move:

    running -> feature -> running -> running -> feature -> running

not:

    viewport -> viewport -> viewport -> recruiter requests extraction


============================================================
08. EDITORIAL SPINE + FOUR COMPOSITION FAMILIES
============================================================

THE BASE LAYER — EDITORIAL SPINE

The Editorial Spine is the continuous structure beneath every case study. It
preserves a stable place for narrative, context, evidence, captions, and expanded
visuals so the reader always knows where to return after an interruption.

The spine is not a fifth expressive composition family. It is the operating
system. The four families below are feature or immersive behaviors applied
selectively inside it.

A. MONUMENT / POSTER

One phrase, question, quote, or metric dominates the scene.

Best for:
- project thesis
- chapter transition
- outcome
- major reframe
- 404, footer, or other shell moments


B. STAGE

An atmospheric outer field contains one controlled inner canvas.

Best for:
- complex UX artifacts
- research synthesis
- system diagrams
- product explanation
- a hero datum with supporting evidence


C. COLLISION

Typography, imagery, and/or artifacts occupy the same physical space.

Best for:
- project transitions
- split digital / real-world narratives
- About page
- major quotes
- participant or contextual photography

Overlap must reinforce the idea. It is not permission to reduce legibility.


D. EXHIBITION / SCATTER

A small number of artifacts are deliberately placed across a large field.

Best for:
- UI showcase
- research materials
- journal or keepsake artifacts
- project index
- Playground

This is not a masonry gallery. Distance, scale, and placement create the story.

COMPOSITION RULE

Monument, Stage, Collision, and Exhibition should interrupt the magazine, not
replace it. The surrounding article must remain perceptually connected before,
during, and after the feature.


============================================================
09. GRID AND RESPONSIVE SYSTEM
============================================================

MASTER GRID

- Wide desktop: 12-column editorial grid
- Standard desktop: remain 12 columns with tighter compositions
- Tablet: 8-column simplified editorial grid
- Mobile: 4-column utility grid
- Narrow mobile: single-column reading flow

Current desktop foundation:

- fluid grid
- 48px outer margins
- 24px gutters
- no fixed desktop max-width
- local reading measures preserved for long-form text

EDITORIAL SPINE ZONES

- outer gutter: chapter marker, progress, or sparse global metadata
- context rail: dates, participant IDs, methods, sources, and figure labels
- main reading column: claim, reasoning, interpretation, and decisions
- evidence rail: quote, metric, annotation, or concise supporting context
- expanded field: screens, photographs, diagrams, charts, and staged artifacts

The main reading column is the anchor. Supporting elements may occupy adjacent
zones or expand across the grid, but the reader should be able to find the
narrative again without searching.

ONLINE READING RULE

Do not flow body copy through multiple newspaper columns. Print can ask the eye
to jump upward and sideways. A scrolling interface should preserve one primary
vertical reading path.

CONTINUITY RULES

- ordinary units size to content; they do not default to 100vh
- do not use scroll snapping in case-study bodies
- preserve the established spacing rhythm between related units
- allow the next heading, image, or caption to enter before the current view ends
- keep captions and evidence visually attached to the argument they support
- use recurring rules, routes, fields, or background transitions across modules
- treat most chapter changes as bands, rules, type shifts, or color changes
- reserve full-screen chapter intermissions for genuinely major narrative turns

The grid governs alignment; it does not require every element to fill a tidy
rectangle.

RESPONSIVE PRINCIPLE

Do not shrink a desktop spread. Recompose it.

On smaller screens:

- preserve the dominant idea
- preserve reading order
- convert overlap into sequence when necessary
- reduce decorative interruptions before reducing clarity
- keep type dramatic, but prevent destructive wrapping
- move edge labels into horizontal metadata
- stack evidence beside its interpretation, not several screens away
- preserve generous space without manufacturing endless scroll


============================================================
10. TYPOGRAPHY
============================================================

Typography carries much of the art direction.

ROLES

DISPLAY / NARRATIVE
- hero language
- project titles
- chapter theses
- turning points
- selected quotes and metrics

BODY / PRODUCT
- reasoning
- product explanation
- navigation
- controls
- captions
- evidence interpretation

MICRO / METADATA
- dates
- participant IDs
- role and scope
- units
- figure labels
- annotations
- evidence source

ACCENT / HUMAN MARK
- sparse handwritten notes
- arrows
- asides
- optional personality

The accent face must never carry comprehension-critical content.

Use scale contrast aggressively, but use tiny type only where the information
is genuinely secondary. Body copy remains comfortably readable.

Typeface assignments belong to the typography source of truth. Current system:

- Salute: primary display
- Gloock: display fallback if licensing requires it
- Figtree: body, UI, metadata, labels, and diagrams
- Plus Jakarta Sans: body/UI fallback
- Esthentic DEMO: sparse accent
- Baby Boston: accent alternative

Belaine has been reconsidered for the hero but is not yet a locked replacement
for Salute. Do not silently change the global system based on one attractive
mockup. That is how font coups happen.


============================================================
11. COLOR SYSTEM
============================================================

CANONICAL BRAND COLORS

- Primary / Deep Magenta: #A13F68
- Secondary / Indigo: #3D3A66
- Accent / Antique Gold: #8C6018

ATMOSPHERIC EDITORIAL FIELDS

- Lilac: #C5B8D6
- Atmospheric Blue: #B4C7D9
- Atmospheric Pink: #DDB9C5
- Butter: #EBD9A4
- Apricot: #F2B07F

COLOR BEHAVIOR

- Use Ink, Ground, one atmospheric field, and one brand accent as a common
  composition limit.
- Deploy the full palette across the portfolio, not inside every viewport.
- Use color semantically and compositionally, not as confetti.
- Project palettes may bend the shell, but navigation and hierarchy remain
  recognizable.
- Never depend on color alone for hierarchy, state, or meaning.

Atmosphere should feel warm, expressive, modern, and slightly unexpected.
Avoid beige luxury cosplay, generic startup gradients, and rainbow soup.


============================================================
12. IMAGERY AND ARTIFACT TREATMENT
============================================================

PHOTOGRAPHY

- allow large, cinematic crops
- crop for narrative tension, not merely subject visibility
- let images enter or leave the viewport when continuation matters
- use high-contrast photography when it strengthens the composition
- give full images later when an earlier crop intentionally withholds context
- do not depend on photography to create every change in rhythm
- let diagrams, UI, research fragments, typography, and data perform equivalent
  editorial roles in evidence-heavy case studies

PRODUCT SCREENS

- show them large enough to inspect
- annotate only the decision-relevant region
- avoid endless device mockups
- avoid three identical cards merely because three screens exist
- use an exhibition or stage when physical presence matters

RESEARCH MATERIAL

- treat evidence as material: excerpts, fragments, patterns, annotations
- keep source and interpretation distinct
- connect evidence visibly to the decision it caused
- avoid decorative quote cards, affinity-map wallpaper, and persona theater

DATA

- metrics may become visual protagonists
- pair every metric with what it means
- avoid generic KPI-card rows
- allow one hero analytical visual to inherit the project's graphic metaphor
- preserve quantitative accuracy and readable labels

CONTINUOUS ARTICLE CADENCE

A common passage may use:

    conclusion-led subheading
    -> two or three short paragraphs
    -> quote or metric entering from the evidence rail
    -> expanded visual crossing several columns
    -> caption and interpretation
    -> narrative resumes on the same spine

This is a pattern, not a mandatory template. Vary the rhythm without abandoning
the reading path.


============================================================
13. MOTION AND INTERACTION
============================================================

MOTION PRINCIPLE

    ALIVE, NOT BUSY.

Motion should do at least one of four jobs:

- REVEAL: bring information into focus
- TRANSITION: connect narrative spaces
- TRANSFORM: show relationships between states
- ORIENT: explain where content came from or where it went

Use motion for:

- chapter transitions
- before / after state changes
- progressive diagrams
- relationship reveals
- artifact entrances when sequence matters
- project preview reactions
- subtle atmospheric movement
- typographic or image transformations tied to meaning

Avoid:

- scroll hijacking
- cursor obstacles
- permanent floating clutter
- mandatory intro sequences
- motion that delays comprehension
- decoration that exists only because it can move
- effects that fail without a fallback

Respect reduced-motion preferences. The meaning must survive if motion fails.

SIGNATURE BRAND INTERACTION

The HOA logomark may spin on hover while the pronunciation /hwa/ reveals from
left to right near the wordmark at 50% of the system Ink color. The delay should
feel intentional and discovery-based, not sluggish. Keyboard focus receives an
equivalent state; reduced motion removes the spin but preserves the label.


============================================================
14. PROJECT-SPECIFIC ART DIRECTION
============================================================

COACHCARE — CONTROLLED SYSTEMS

Role in portfolio:
Flagship proof of professional maturity, systems thinking, prioritization, and
design within a constrained product ecosystem.

Visual behavior:

- most disciplined case-study world
- Product Clarity and Stage dominate
- crisp UI, system diagrams, measured hierarchy
- information density is allowed, but staged calmly
- restrained palette and sparse expressive moments
- show consequence and operational logic before visual polish

Working graphic metaphor:

    fragmented work -> coordinated action

Possible recurring material:

- rails
- connected states
- task / signal paths
- controlled modular fields

Avoid generic healthcare gradients, a dashboard collage, or making mature work
look less serious in order to prove the portfolio has personality.


SUMMER QUEST — ACCUMULATED CITY MEMORY

Role in portfolio:
Strongest proof of full ownership, behavioral design, shipping, live learning,
and the relationship between a digital tool and a real-world experience.

Visual behavior:

- most expressive case study
- Statement, Collision, and Exhibition used confidently
- NYC photography may dominate entire beats
- illustrated map fragments, journal artifacts, quest imagery, and participant
  evidence should share one world
- charts should feel native to the experience, not like analytics software
  wearing lipstick

Graphic metaphor:

    individual paths accumulate into a shared city memory

Recurring material may include:

- routes and connective lines
- dots and time markers
- overlapping participant paths
- map fragments
- image interruptions
- layered real-world and digital evidence

Use the motif to represent movement, sequence, social connection, different
journeys, and accumulated memory. Do not sprinkle unrelated cute symbols merely
to make the page playful.

The portfolio system governs presentation and structure. The Summer Quest
system governs project-specific color, illustration, photography, and flavor.


PERFORMAMATCH — EVIDENCE BECOMES A MODEL

Role in portfolio:
Proof of foundational research, pattern recognition, synthesis, and strategic
product direction.

Visual behavior:

- investigative and editorial
- restrained atmosphere
- evidence fragments become structured patterns
- quotes, diagrams, and models carry more weight than final UI
- annotations remain precise and source-aware

Graphic metaphor:

    fragments -> patterns -> product direction

Use progressive structure to show how raw evidence changed the model of the
problem. Avoid detective-board cosplay, theatrical red strings, or pretending
research is rigorous merely because it has many sticky notes.


ABOUT — PERSONALITY WITH EVIDENCE

- use the locked 25% Observer / 35% Maker / 40% Curious Cat framing
- allow spatial typography, image interruption, and more personal motion
- show interests as evidence of how Kathy notices and makes, not as a hobby list
- keep professional history scannable; the current CoachCare role may open by
  default in the experience accordion


PLAYGROUND — CONTROLLED FREEDOM

- may use the highest shell-level experimentation
- each project can have its own visual behavior
- labels and entry points remain obvious
- experiments should reinforce curiosity, culture, behavior, or making
- do not let breadth dilute the three core case studies


============================================================
15. HOMEPAGE DIRECTION
============================================================

The homepage is the Projects page. It should not add a redundant index before
the work.

CURRENT HIERARCHY

1. CoachCare — dominant flagship
2. Summer Quest — expressive secondary project
3. PerformaMatch — concise research-led teaser

Do not place the projects in identical cards.

Use asymmetric editorial staging while preserving a fast scan:

- quiet universal header
- clear HOA identity
- immediate Product Designer signal
- strong CoachCare dominance
- project worlds that feel distinct
- concise teaser copy
- clear click targets

The user should identify Kathy as a Product Designer and find at least two
clickable projects within approximately five seconds.


============================================================
16. CASE-STUDY STRUCTURE AND NAVIGATION
============================================================

The visual system must support:

    claim -> evidence -> interpretation -> decision -> result

Case studies should behave as continuous online articles, not slide decks laid
vertically. Several related ideas may remain visible and connected within the
same flow. Section boundaries clarify the argument; they should not repeatedly
reset the reader's attention.

Case studies may use a compact chapter rail when the narrative has real chapter
structure. The rail must orient, not decorate.

Summer Quest working example:

    01 ORIGIN
    02 PARTICIPATION
    03 GAME
    04 REALITY
    05 AFTER

Desktop may show the full rail. Mobile may compress to:

    03 / 05  GAME

Chapter openings should use conclusion-led language, not generic process labels
such as Research, Ideation, and Final Design.

The default chapter transition is an editorial change of pace, not a new full
screen. Use an immersive chapter opening only when the chapter contains a real
narrative turn that deserves one.


============================================================
17. EVIDENCE STANDARD
============================================================

Every major visual should perform one of these jobs:

- reveal a problem
- show evidence
- explain a system
- compare meaningful options
- document a decision
- show an iteration caused by learning
- show an outcome
- establish necessary context

Research should visibly move through:

    EVIDENCE
    -> INTERPRETATION
    -> NEW MODEL
    -> PRODUCT CONSEQUENCE

Before / after is valuable only when it proves changed thinking. Do not display
six nearly identical screens as though pixel archaeology is a leadership skill.


============================================================
18. VOICE, LABELS, AND ANNOTATION
============================================================

The portfolio voice should feel:

- warm
- curious
- observant
- direct
- evidence-aware
- occasionally witty

Micro labels should feel technical and precise, creating contrast with
expressive display language. Tiny text is reserved for metadata, not body copy.

Captions should explain:

- what the reader is looking at
- why it matters
- what changed because of it

Avoid captions that merely rename the image.


============================================================
19. ACCESSIBILITY AND USABILITY
============================================================

Expressive disruption does not excuse basic failure.

Required:

- readable body size and line length
- strong color contrast
- visible keyboard focus
- meaningful DOM reading order
- alt text for informative imagery
- reduced-motion support
- touch targets that remain obvious on mobile
- no essential information hidden behind hover
- no overlap that destroys comprehension
- no interaction that traps scrolling or focus

The utility layer should be boring in the best possible way: reliable.


============================================================
20. REFERENCE ROLES
============================================================

COMMERCIAL / CREATIVE-AGENCY REFERENCES

Use these to study concentrated composition:

- spatial typography
- extreme hierarchy
- staged artifacts
- collision and cropping
- graphic-system thinking
- memorable transitions

They are not the structural model for the full case-study body. Their lighter
content and image supply permit more viewport-scale staging than a hiring case
study can sustain.


EDITORIAL / MAGAZINE REFERENCES

Use these to study continuity:

- stable article spine
- changing image widths
- pull quotes embedded inside an argument
- sidebars and context rails
- recurring captions and metadata
- visual rhythm without repeated resets

Tejas Kumar's story is useful for executive summary, contents, orientation,
progress, and recurring chapter grammar. Its contemplative scrollytelling pace
and large empty fields are not the target density for recruiter reading.

The HypeMag-style long-form article is useful for elegant pacing, split openings,
inline pull quotes, and image/body relationships, but still depends heavily on
portrait photography.

The D2-style editorial pages are the strongest continuity reference: one article
spine supports body copy, subheads, pull quotes, captions, side context, and
several image scales without turning each item into its own scene.

Take the reading logic, not print-sized typography or multi-column body flow.


============================================================
21. WHAT THIS DIRECTION IS NOT
============================================================

Do not copy:

- luxury real-estate darkness
- beige serif prestige signaling
- raster mountains
- pseudo-technical labels with no meaning
- unreadably tiny body copy
- fixed desktop spreads on mobile
- newspaper-style multi-column body copy on screen
- full-screen treatment for ordinary paragraphs or every chapter opening
- case studies assembled as vertically stacked slides
- huge empty fields around evidence that should be read together
- pull quotes that merely repeat nearby copy without creating emphasis
- constant WebGL spectacle
- generic KPI cards
- four matching insight cards
- card grids for every type of content
- decorative UX artifacts
- motion without a communication job
- weirdness used to conceal weak hierarchy
- a generic colorful Product Designer template

The references are valuable because of their composition logic, not because
Kathy needs to cosplay as a 30-person creative agency.


============================================================
22. REVIEW CHECKLIST
============================================================

For every major composition, ask:

1. What is the dominant idea?
2. What should a recruiter understand in three seconds?
3. What is the content function: Statement, Editorial Evidence, or Product Clarity?
4. What is the presentation scale: Running, Feature, or Immersive?
5. Has this idea earned its visual scale?
6. Does the unit preserve the Editorial Spine before and after it?
7. Which composition family, if any, is doing the work?
8. Is the grid being broken deliberately or merely inconsistently?
9. Does the artifact prove a decision, behavior, or result?
10. Is the visual metaphor native to this project?
11. Is motion revealing, transitioning, transforming, or orienting?
12. Does the composition still work in black and white?
13. Does the mobile version preserve the idea rather than miniaturize it?
14. Is any important information available only on hover?
15. Could one element be removed without losing meaning?

If the answer to the last question is yes, remove it. The Empire already has
enough unnecessary machinery.


============================================================
23. FINAL PRINCIPLE
============================================================

The portfolio should repeatedly make one thing obvious:

    Kathy notices something important,
    forms a point of view,
    makes a deliberate choice,
    and can show what happened because of it.

The art direction should make that reasoning more memorable. The Editorial
Spine should make it easier to follow. Neither may make the reasoning harder to
see.
